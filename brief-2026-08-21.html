Panama Canal Slashes Ship Traffic as El Niño Dries Waterway
The Panama Canal is about to restrict the number of vessels that can pass through one of the world's most critical trade routes. That's left water levels in the canal's locks too low to accommodate the normal volume of traffic. The Panama Canal handles about 6% of global maritime trade, so even modest reductions can ripple through supply chains worldwide. Ships that can't get a slot face a choice: wait for days or weeks, or take the much longer route around South America's southern tip. The timing is particularly tough for global shipping, which is already dealing with higher fuel costs and tight schedules. The canal uses freshwater from nearby lakes to operate its lock system, and those reservoirs depend on consistent rainfall. El Niño patterns have disrupted that rainfall, leaving operators with no choice but to reduce the daily number of transits. It's a stark reminder of how climate patterns can choke off global commerce at a single chokepoint.

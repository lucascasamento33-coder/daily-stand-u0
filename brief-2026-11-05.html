East Asia's being battered by an unusually fierce typhoon season that meteorologists are linking to El Niño. The result has been more frequent typhoons and greater intensity when they make landfall. Coastal communities across the region have faced repeated evacuations, flooding, and infrastructure damage over a span of weeks rather than the typical pattern of isolated storms. Climate researchers warn that El Niño events—which occur naturally but are being amplified by overall ocean warming—are making extreme weather more common across the Pacific rim. For millions of people in the region, a weather pattern that used to mean an active storm season now means something closer to relentless bombardment.
